Learn

Acids are “compounds that release hydrogen ions (H+) when they dissolve in water.” Their names come from the anion: a binary acid (H plus one other element) is named hydro-…-ic acid, and an oxoacid takes its name from its oxoanion, with -ate becoming -ic and -ite becoming -ous.

What we observe

Hydrogen chloride, HCl, is a molecular compound. Dissolved in water, it makes the solution called hydrochloric acid.

What we write

HCl(aq), hydrochloric acid. HBr is hydrogen bromide, but HBr(aq) is hydrobromic acid. H2SO4, sulfuric acid (from sulfate); HNO2, nitrous acid (from nitrite).

What the particles are doing

“In that aqueous solution, every molecule of HCl has separated into a H+ ion and a Cl− ion.” An oxoacid combines H+ ions with an oxoanion, enough of them to balance its charge.

Source: textbook §4.3, Binary Acids and Oxoacids, PDF p.193–195 (printed 159–161). Not yet taught in lecture.

H count = the size of the anion's charge

Formula One H+ for each negative charge: HNO3 (nitrate, 1−), H2SO4 (sulfate, 2−), H3PO4 (phosphate, 3−).

The (aq) label “To distinguish between a hydrogen halide (a molecular compound with covalent bonds…) and the aqueous form (H+ ions and X− ions dissolved in water), we add the symbol (aq)” (PDF p.194).

The chlorine family (Table 4.5, PDF p.192): hypochlorite → hypochlorous acid, chlorite → chlorous acid, chlorate → chloric acid, perchlorate → perchloric acid. The prefixes stay; only the ending changes.

Some roots grow when the ending changes: sulfate → sulfuric, sulfite → sulfurous, phosphate → phosphoric (textbook Sample Ex. 4.8).

Connected lecture ideas

The oxoanions come from the polyatomic-ion table the professor provides on exams (Day 8 p.8), and the H count is the same charge balance as for any ionic formula (Day 7 p.20). The acid names themselves haven't been taught.

Recognize it

  • A formula starting with H, written (aq) or called an acid → acid naming.
  • No oxygen → a binary acid: hydro-…-ic. Oxygen → name it from the oxoanion's ending.
  • Look-alikes: HCl is hydrogen chloride (the molecular compound); HCl(aq) is hydrochloric acid. HCO3− (hydrogen carbonate) is an anion, not an acid name.

Common mistakes

  • Putting hydro- on an oxoacid (“hydrosulfuric” for H2SO4).
  • Swapping -ic and -ous (chlorate → “chlorous”).
  • Naming the (aq) solution as the molecule (“hydrogen bromide”).
  • Using the wrong number of H atoms for the anion's charge.
