What the labels mean

Covered in lecture

Taught on the Day 1–8 slides. Each block cites the slide, for example “Day 4 p.10.” The professor's wording, symbols, and methods are used, such as φ for the work function and u for velocity.

Textbook preview

In Gilbert Ch. 1–4 but not yet taught in lecture (through Day 8). It's here so you can read ahead. It is never presented as something your professor said, and whether it will be tested is unknown.

Background (not from lecture) marks an outside explanation or value added for understanding, such as literature work-function values.

Connection marks a link the guide infers between topics. It's reasoning, not a quote.

How each section works

  1. Learn: the big idea, told three ways: what you observe, what you write, and what the particles are doing.
  2. Understand: why the relationship holds, what every symbol means, when to use it, and which look-alike it's easy to confuse it with.
  3. Explore: an interactive model. Change one thing and predict what happens before you look.
  4. Attempt: one problem before any worked example. Use the hints one at a time.
  5. Compare: the tempting wrong reasoning next to the correct reasoning.
  6. Practice: problems that fade the scaffolding.
  7. Master: a transfer problem, an explanation to write in your own words, a recognition question, and a sanity check.

Answers are checked to ±1%, with separate notes on units and significant figures. Your progress is saved in this browser only. The line spectrum at the top brightens as you master each section, and “Reset progress” in the sidebar clears it.
