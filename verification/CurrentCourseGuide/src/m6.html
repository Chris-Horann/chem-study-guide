Learn

Bohr explained hydrogen's lines by quantizing the atom itself: the electron can occupy only certain orbits, each with a fixed energy. When it drops from a higher level to a lower one, the energy lost leaves as a single photon, so each line is one jump.

What we observe

Hydrogen's four visible lines (Day 3 p.9), plus ultraviolet and infrared series. Balmer's empirical formula even predicted new lines, “lines that nobody knew existed in the ultraviolet” (Day 4 p.7).

What we write

Balmer: λ = 364.56 nm × n2/(n2 − 4) (Day 3 p.21). Rydberg: 1/λ = RH(1/n12 − 1/n22) (Day 4 p.7). Bohr: ΔE = −2.178 × 10−18 J (1/nfinal2 − 1/ninitial2) (Day 4 p.10).

What the electron is doing

It sits in level n with energy −2.178 × 10−18 J/n2. Levels crowd together toward n = ∞, where the electron is free. Dropping to n = 1 gives UV (Lyman), to n = 2 visible (Balmer), to n = 3 IR (Paschen).

Source: Day 3 p.21; Day 4 p.3, p.6–10.

Understand

ΔE = −2.178 × 10−18 J (1/nfinal2 − 1/ninitial2)

n
positive integers; n = ∞ means the electron is removed (ionization)
ΔE
change in the atom's energy, J per atom

Sign Emission (nfinal < ninitial) makes ΔE negative: the atom loses energy. Absorption makes it positive. The photon carries |ΔE| = hc/λ (Day 4 p.10).

Limits “TRUE for hydrogen and other one-electron atoms … once even a second electron is involved, the Bohr model falls apart” (Day 4 p.3).

Ionization from n = 1 ΔE = +2.178 × 10−18 J per atom; × NA gives 1312 kJ/mol, hydrogen's IE₁ on Day 7 p.9.

Bohr's quantized orbits get a physical reason in §3.5: a whole number of de Broglie wavelengths must fit around the orbit (Day 4 p.15–16).

Recognize it

  • Hydrogen (or a one-electron ion) and two n values → ΔE, then λ = hc/|ΔE|.
  • A transition ending on n = 2 in the visible → Balmer's formula also works.
  • A multi-electron atom's spectrum → Bohr does not apply.

Common mistakes

  • Using 1/n instead of 1/n².
  • Swapping nfinal and ninitial (sign error).
  • Forgetting ΔE is per atom (× NA for per mole).
  • Applying Bohr to helium.
