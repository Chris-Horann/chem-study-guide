Learn

An electron configuration lists where all of an atom's electrons are, using three rules. Aufbau: fill the lowest-energy orbitals first. Pauli: at most two electrons per orbital, with opposite spins. Hund: spread electrons across degenerate orbitals before pairing them. The energy order comes from penetration, and it puts 4s below 3d.

What we observe

Elements in a column behave alike; the periodic table's blocks (s, p, d, f) mirror the subshell being filled (Day 6 p.20).

What we write

Notation n ℓ# (Day 6 p.6): Li 1s22s1 = [He]2s1 (Day 6 p.14); C [He]2s22p2 with 2p boxes [↑][↑][ ] (Day 6 p.16); Ni [Ar]4s23d8 in filling order, or [Ar]3d84s2 in n order as written on Day 6 p.21 (both orders appear in lecture).

What the electrons are doing

They repel, so in degenerate orbitals they spread out with parallel spins before pairing (“We will refrain from ‘pairing’ electrons until we HAVE to,” Day 6 p.16). Core electrons shield the valence electrons; the valence electrons do the chemistry (Day 6 p.14).

Source: Day 6 p.6–20; Day 7 p.6.

Procedure Count electrons from Z → fill in ladder order, 2 per orbital (s 2, p 6, d 10) → apply Hund within a subshell → optionally write the filled core as [noble gas].

Penetration and shielding (Day 6 p.12–13) Li's 2s electron sees the 3+ nucleus through the 1s2 core as roughly Zeff ≈ 1+. The penetrating 2s lies below 2p, which is why Li is 1s22s1.

Scope statement (Day 7 p.6)

“It turns out that some atoms (almost always transition metals) exhibit electron configurations that are NOT the ones we would predict … We will completely ignore this in this class, because the transition metals don't crop up that often. But the book talks about it.” Every problem in this guide uses the rules.

Textbook preview (§3.8, PDF p.149–152, printed 115–118)

Ground vs. excited states: a sodium atom in a flame absorbs energy and moves its 3s electron to 3p ([Ne]3p1, the first excited state). Falling back emits sodium's yellow-orange light.

The exceptions the book talks about: Cr [Ar]3d54s1 (half-filled 3d), Cu [Ar]3d104s1 (filled 3d), and Ag [Kr]4d105s1. The textbook writes valence orbitals in n order, and it extends the ladder into the f block, e.g., Pb [Xe]4f145d106s26p2. For this course, remember that the professor said to ignore the exceptions.

Recognize it

  • “Write the configuration” or “how many unpaired electrons” → ladder plus Hund.
  • Past Ar → 4s before 3d.
  • Its last electron enters 3d → a d-block (transition) element.
