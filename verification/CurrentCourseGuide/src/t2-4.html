Learn

The atomic mass printed in the periodic table is a weighted average over an element's isotopes: each isotope's mass counts in proportion to its natural abundance. No single atom has that average mass. The same bookkeeping, summed over the atoms in a formula, gives molecular and formula masses.

Covered in lecture (RAMP UP slides)

“Atomic mass units (amu) are the unit used to express the relative masses of atoms and subatomic particles. 1 amu is equal to 1/12 of the mass of a carbon atom (specifically, a carbon-12 atom).” “1 amu = 1 dalton (Da)” (Day 2 p.21, p.23). The textbook's name for the unit is the unified atomic mass unit (u), the same unit.

Textbook preview (§2.4, PDF p.94–98, printed 60–64)

What we observe

Neon is 90.4838% 20Ne, 0.2696% 21Ne, and 9.2465% 22Ne (textbook Table 2.3).

What we write

mNe = 19.9924(0.904838) + 20.9940(0.002696) + 21.9914(0.092465) = 20.1799 u (Eq. 2.3).

What the particles are doing

Every neon atom has one of the three isotope masses. A macroscopic sample contains them all in natural proportions, so it behaves as if each atom weighed 20.18 u.

Understand

Textbook preview

mX = a1m1 + a2m2 + a3m3 + …

mi
mass of isotope i (amu)
ai
natural abundance as a decimal (the ai sum to 1)

Carry one extra digit through the products; round the sum to justified significant figures.

Two-isotope reverse problem Let x be one abundance and 1 − x the other: mX = x·m1 + (1 − x)m2, then solve for x.

Check The average must lie between the lightest and heaviest isotope masses, closer to the more abundant one.

Textbook preview

molecular mass = Σ (atoms of each element × average atomic mass)

SO2: 32.065 + 2(15.999) = 64.063 u. An ionic compound has no molecules, so its formula unit (e.g., one Ca2+ and two Cl−) has a formula mass: 40.078 + 2(35.453) = 110.984 u. Gaining or losing electrons barely changes an atom's mass.

Natural abundances vary a little with the source; a seawater sample in the textbook gave chlorine a mass of 35.455 u. The textbook's own two-isotope sample problem (Ag) has an internal slip: it states 107.87 u but solves with 107.89 u. The problems here are new and don't reuse it.

Common mistakes

  • Taking a simple average of the isotope masses.
  • Using percents instead of decimals (answers 100× too big).
  • Calling an ionic formula mass a molecular mass.
